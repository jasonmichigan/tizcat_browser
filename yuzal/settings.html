AI

Claude takes all five levels. A model on this PC has one dial: anything above low turns its thinking on, which is two to three times slower, and a model with no thinking mode is left alone. default means no thinking for a local model: it is the setting most of the waiting used to come from.

One step is: read the page, decide, act. Yuzal stops at this many even if the task is unfinished. It is the backstop against a loop that never ends.

Yuzal may navigate, type, attach files and click through a multi-page form, and then stops at the final button: Submit, Apply Now, Pay. It leaves the form filled and tells you it is ready. Leave this on unless you want it finishing things on your behalf.

Read-only lets Yuzal open pages, read them and scroll. Every action that could change something (click, type, submit, write a file) is refused, and the refusal is recorded. The check runs where the action is executed, not in the instructions given to the model.

What Yuzal remembers between sessions

The models never learn. Nothing is trained on your browsing, and clearing the chat wipes the conversation. Everything below is simply re-read at the start of every answer, which is why its size matters: it is sent again each time.

On, it keeps things you mention in passing (what you are working on, a standing preference, what one of your words refers to) without being asked, and says in one line what it kept. Off, it records only what you explicitly tell it to remember. Either way it never keeps a password, and never keeps what a web page claims about you.

Layer 1: stable facts

Things that stay true: which university you are at, that you want answers in metric, what you mean by “the dashboard”. Kept until you remove them.

Layer 2: dated notes

Things true of a particular day: “that deadline was 15 Oct”. Recorded with the date, shown to the model as possibly out of date, and discarded automatically after the expiry below.
